Lean of peak technique (Savvy Aviation)

1. Climb: lean in the climb above about 3,000 ft so the hottest CHT stays at or below 380°F.
2. Level off: set the throttle wide open or nearly so, and RPM 2300 to 2400.
3. Big pull: pull the mixture briskly well past peak until the engine just starts to lose smoothness, so you never linger in the red box.
4. Set power with fuel flow: enrich slowly until smooth, then set the gph shown below for the power you want.
5. Fly by CHT: keep the hottest CHT at or below 380°F. Above 380, lean a bit more or reduce MP. Above 400, act right away.
6. Going back rich: push the mixture briskly through peak to well rich; don't stop around 50°F rich of peak.
Carbureted engine: your O-540 has a carburetor, so cylinder to cylinder mixture may be uneven. If it runs rough before every cylinder is lean of peak, that's mixture spread, not a bad engine. Record a lean test on your engine monitor and upload it to Savvy Aviation for analysis.
Lean of peak is not covered by your POH, which calls for best power or peak EGT at 75% power or less. It needs an engine monitor with CHT and EGT on every cylinder, plus a fuel flow gauge.
